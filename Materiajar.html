7 to 10. Grammar Focus

POSSESSIVE ADJECTIVES

Possessive adjectives are used to show ownership, relationship, or connection.
Rule: Possessive Adjective + Noun (e.g., my mother).

Subject Pronoun Possessive Adjective Example in a Sentence
ImyMy mother is a teacher.
YouyourWhat is your brother's name?
HehisHis father is a doctor.
SheherHer sister is a student.
ItitsThe cat is with its owner.
WeourOur family is happy.
TheytheirTheir parents are teachers.

DON'T CONFUSE THEM!

HE → HIS (Male) He is my brother.
His name is Andi.
SHE → HER (Female) She is my sister.
Her name is Rina.
THEY → THEIR (Plural) They are my parents.
Their house is in Banggai.

7–14. Grammar Focus — Simple Present Tense

The Simple Present Tense is used to talk about:

✓ daily routines; ✓ habits; ✓ repeated activities; ✓ general facts.

I go to school every day.
She studies English every morning.
They play football after school.
He helps his parents at home.
I / YOU / WE / THEY
Subject + Verb 1 + Object

I study English every day.

You go to school every morning.

We play football after school.

They watch television at night.

HE / SHE / IT
Subject + Verb 1 + s/es + Object

He studies English every day.

She goes to school every morning.

He plays football after school.

She watches television at night.

Verb Changes

play → plays
read → reads
work → works
eat → eats
go → goes
do → does
watch → watches
wash → washes
study → studies
have → has
Important: I have breakfast. But: She has breakfast.

Negative Sentences

I / You / We / They + do not + Verb 1

He / She / It + does not + Verb 1

I do not watch television every day.

He does not play football every afternoon.

She doesn't goes to school. → She doesn't go to school.

Questions

Do + I/you/we/they + Verb 1...?

Does + he/she/it + Verb 1...?

Do you go to school every day? — Yes, I do.

Does she study English every day? — Yes, she does.

Do they play football after school? — No, they don't.

Does he help his parents? — Yes, he does.

04 • Grammar

Be Going To — Future Plans 🚀

Use be going to + base verb to talk about plans or intentions that you have already decided.

Affirmative

Subject + am/is/are + going to + verb

I am going to study.

She is going to travel.

They are going to play.

Negative

Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + verb

I am not going to stay home.

He isn't going to come.

Question

Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + verb?

Are you going to study?

Is she going to travel?

Remember: After going to, use the base form of the verb: going to study, going to visit, going to play.

07 • Reading

Our Weekend Plan 📖

This weekend, my family is going to spend time together. On Saturday morning, we are going to clean the house. After lunch, my brother and I are going to play badminton. In the afternoon, we are going to visit our aunt. She lives near the beach. On Sunday, we are going to have breakfast together and then I am going to practice English for my next class. I am excited because we are going to do useful activities and enjoy time with our family.

Think & Discuss

  1. 1. What is the family going to do on Saturday morning?
  2. 2. What are the writer and brother going to play?
  3. 3. Who are they going to visit?
  4. 4. What is the writer going to practice on Sunday?
  5. 5. Why is the writer excited?

Notice: is going to spend • are going to clean • are going to play • are going to visit • am going to practice

10 • Deep Thinking

Plans, Choices & Responsibility 💭

Planning is not only about saying what we want to do. A good plan helps us use time wisely, prepare for challenges, and think about how our choices affect ourselves and others.

Purpose

Preparation

Responsibility

Who may be affected by my plan?

Think deeper: “A plan can change, but having a clear purpose helps us choose our next step.”
